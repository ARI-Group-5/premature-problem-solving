# Internal Calibration Results

## Calibration Method

Before beginning full annotation, all five team members independently labeled the same 20 Context–Response pairs using the preliminary **YES**, **NO**, and **SKIP** definitions.

Team members completed their labels without reviewing one another’s answers. This process produced 100 individual judgments.

> **Important:** These results describe the team’s preliminary internal calibration. They are not the project’s final inter-annotator agreement results or final ground-truth labels.

## Agreement Results

| Result | Number of cases | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Unanimous agreement | 11 | 55% |
| 4–1 agreement | 3 | 15% |
| 3–2 agreement | 6 | 30% |
| **Total** | **20** | **100%** |

Across the 100 individual judgments:

| Label | Number of judgments |
|---|---:|
| YES | 39 |
| NO | 60 |
| SKIP | 1 |
| **Total** | **100** |

The average pairwise agreement among team members was approximately **76%**. This indicates meaningful agreement while also showing that several parts of the preliminary annotation guidelines require clarification before full-scale annotation begins.

The majority label for a calibration case should not be interpreted as the correct or final label. The team will review and adjudicate the non-unanimous cases before assigning final calibration labels.

## Sources of Disagreement

Disagreements were concentrated around several recurring questions:

- Whether recognizing symptoms counts as emotional acknowledgment
- Whether phrases such as “it sounds like” provide sufficient acknowledgment or validation
- Whether normalization counts as acknowledgment
- Whether a causal explanation of someone’s feelings counts as acknowledgment
- Whether praising someone for seeking help sufficiently acknowledges the emotional concern
- How urgent safety guidance should be handled in crisis or self-harm examples
- How much acknowledgment is necessary before advice begins
- When an item should receive SKIP rather than YES or NO

These disagreements show that the task requires annotators to interpret acknowledgment quality and response sequence rather than identify individual keywords.

## Annotation Timing

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---:|
| Annotators | 5 |

| Total judgments | 100 |
| Combined annotation time | 101 minutes |
| Average time per annotator | 20.2 minutes |
| Average time per judgment | 60.6 seconds |
| Estimated judgments per person-hour | Approximately 59 |

At the observed rate, producing two independent annotations for 1,200 records would require approximately **40.4 total annotation hours**. This estimate excludes calibration, adjudication, breaks, technical issues, and quality review.

The timing results will help the team select a realistic annotation subset and determine the size of the shared agreement set and distributed remainder.

## Interpretation

The calibration supports the feasibility of the proposed task, but it also shows that the preliminary guidelines should not yet be treated as final.

The calibration identified nine non-unanimous cases:

- Three cases with 4–1 agreement
- Six cases with 3–2 agreement

These cases will be reviewed by the team to determine why annotators applied the rules differently and whether the guidelines adequately address each situation.
